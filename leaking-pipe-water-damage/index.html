Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Union City

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Union City finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Pipes punctured by a screw or nail, often during a renovation or when mounting shelves.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Decayed Wall Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Flooring Damage

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Wet Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Drying equipment stays in place, typically for 3 to 5 days, until moisture in the framing and subfloor is back to normal. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.
