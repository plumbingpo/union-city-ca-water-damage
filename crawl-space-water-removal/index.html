Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Union City

Water under the house is easy to miss. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • A look through the hatch shows pooled water or soupy soil
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Pumping out the water usually takes a few hours. The wood needs more time, most often 3 to 5 days of air movers and a dehumidifier working below the floor. Thick beams, a dirt floor with no liner, or water that sat for weeks can add time. Meter readings decide when we are done, rather than a set number of days.
Going in while water is pooled is a risk we advise against. Many homes have electrical lines in that space, so the water itself may be live. There can be sewage or bacteria from the ground in it too, plus tight quarters and stale air. Looking in from the hatch with a flashlight is fine, then call us to inspect.
Moist ground after a downpour is nothing unusual, though actual puddles are not something to leave alone. Just an inch or two will hold humidity at a level where mold grows and lumber rots. Get it looked at when water lingers a day past the end of the rain or appears during a dry spell.
